# Worked examples

Seven requests taken from first reading to finished prompt. They show the process across different mediums. Read them to calibrate, then make different choices: a new request that comes out as a linocut with one yellow accent has copied an example instead of being art-directed.

Each prompt ends with its format ("Portrait 4:5") because these are written to be pasted into a generator. When you call an image tool that has an aspect-ratio setting, set it there and drop that last phrase.

## Contents

1. An open subject: "a fox"
2. A cover with text: an ambient album called Low Tide
3. An abstract concept: a blog post about burnout
4. A poster with lettering: a neighbourhood jazz night
5. The user's own genre: "a cyberpunk city"
6. Abstract decoration: "something calm and blue for the bedroom"
7. A character: an elderly witch who runs a launderette

## 1. An open subject

**Request:** "Make me an image of a fox."

**Reading:** Subject only, everything else open. One bold interpretation, no questions.

**Idea:** The wild as a commuter. A fox using the zebra crossing at 4 a.m., seen from an upstairs window. (Viewpoint, plus a small joke.)

**Decisions:** Gouache on grey paper. Straight-down view. One streetlamp out of frame. Slate, charcoal and grey with the fox as the only warm colour.

**Prompt:**

> Gouache painting on grey toned paper, flat opaque shapes with dry-brush edges. View straight down from an upstairs window onto an empty suburban street before dawn: parked cars as dark rounded blocks, a zebra crossing, wet tarmac. A small rust-orange fox trots across the crossing, exactly on the stripes, its long shadow thrown by a single streetlamp out of frame. Palette of slate blue, charcoal and soft grey, the fox the only warm colour. Visible brush texture, slightly chalky finish. Still, private, a little funny. Portrait 4:5.

## 2. A cover with text

**Request:** "I need cover art for my ambient album, it's called Low Tide."

**Reading:** A project that matters to them, with required text. Worth offering directions; this is the one they picked.

**Idea:** What the tide left behind. The cover is a contact print of the tideline itself. (Absence.)

**Decisions:** Cyanotype photogram. Objects along the bottom third, the rest empty blue. No light source, because a photogram has none. Prussian blue and white only. Small quiet lettering.

**Prompt:**

> Cyanotype photogram on heavy watercolour paper, deep Prussian blue with white silhouettes. Along the bottom third, a tideline of real objects laid directly on the paper: strands of bladderwrack seaweed, a few small shells, a length of frayed rope, their edges soft where they lifted off the surface. The upper two-thirds is empty blue, with uneven brushed borders and faint water stains. Small, widely letterspaced white capitals in the top-left corner read "LOW TIDE". Still, spacious, slightly cold. Square 1:1.

## 3. An abstract concept

**Request:** "Header image for a blog post about burnout."

**Reading:** A concept, not a subject. Needs a metaphor that reads at thumbnail size, and room for a headline.

**Idea:** One match left. (Metaphor, avoiding the stock candle and battery.)

**Decisions:** Cut-paper collage shot from above. Matchbook in the left third, the rest empty for the headline. Soft overhead light giving real shadows between paper layers. Warm grey, off-white and charcoal, one red match head.

**Prompt:**

> Cut-paper collage photographed from directly above, with soft real shadows between the layers. An open matchbook lies in the left third of a wide frame on a warm grey paper background. Inside it, a row of torn cardboard stubs where the matches used to be, and one match left, leaning slightly, its head the only red in the image. Off-white, warm grey and charcoal papers with visible fibre and slightly uneven scissor cuts. The right two-thirds of the frame stays empty. Dry, quiet, a little rueful. Landscape 16:9.

## 4. A poster with lettering

**Request:** "Poster for our neighbourhood jazz night. It should say JAZZ AT THE CORNER and Thursdays, 8pm."

**Reading:** Exact text given. Graphic work, so a constraint will do more than a scene.

**Idea:** The trumpet's bell is the moon over the neighbourhood. (Constraint: two inks, cut shapes only.)

**Decisions:** Two-colour screenprint, mid-century in spirit. Big tilted shape entering from a corner, rooftops along the bottom. No modelled light at all. Mustard and black on off-white.

**Prompt:**

> Two-colour screenprint poster in mustard yellow and black on off-white stock, mid-century modern in spirit: flat cut-out shapes with clean edges and solid colour. A huge trumpet bell tilts in from the top-right corner and doubles as a full moon over a row of small black rooftops and chimneys along the bottom. Loose black dots and dashes drift from the bell across the sky like notes. Slight ink overlap where the colours meet, light paper grain. Along the bottom in bold hand-cut capitals: "JAZZ AT THE CORNER", and smaller beneath it, "Thursdays, 8pm". Warm, lively, neighbourly. Portrait 2:3.

## 5. The user's own genre

**Request:** "A cyberpunk city."

**Reading:** They chose the genre, so the genre stays: density, technology layered over decay. What is open is everything the genre's clichés usually fill in, which is night, rain and pink-and-teal neon.

**Idea:** The same city at noon, when it is just somewhere people live. (Time, and tone against subject.)

**Decisions:** 35mm film photograph. High walkway looking across a canyon of housing. Midday sun through humid haze. Sun-bleached concrete and pale turquoise with one red cable car.

**Prompt:**

> 35mm film photograph, slightly overexposed, of a dense megacity at midday in thick humid haze. Shot from a high walkway looking across a canyon of stacked housing blocks: air-conditioning units, tangled cables, laundry strung between towers thirty floors up, faded holographic shop signs washed out by the sun. A single bright red cable car crosses the gap in the middle distance. Sun-bleached concrete, pale turquoise and dusty white, the red car the only saturated colour. Fine film grain, soft halation on the highlights. Tired, crowded, ordinary. Landscape 3:2.

**When showing it:** offer the classic neon night as the alternative. Some people asking for cyberpunk want exactly that, and it is their image.

## 6. Abstract decoration

**Request:** "Something abstract and calm for the bedroom wall, I like blues."

**Reading:** Mood and colour given, subject open. Calm is the brief; strangeness would be a mistake here.

**Idea:** The hour when sea and sky are the same colour.

**Decisions:** Wet-on-wet watercolour. Two horizontal fields meeting below the middle. No depicted light; the bare paper is the light. Indigo, grey-blue and a sliver of warm white.

**Prompt:**

> Abstract watercolour on heavy cold-pressed paper, painted wet-on-wet. Two broad horizontal fields of blue, a deep indigo below and a pale grey-blue above, meet in a soft uneven band a little below the middle, where the pigments bleed into each other and granulate. A thin sliver of unpainted warm-white paper runs along that meeting line like the last light on a horizon. Visible paper texture, pigment pooled at the edges, one faint tide mark where a wash dried. The whole image is these two fields and the line between them. Slow, hushed, spacious. Landscape 3:2.

## 7. A character

**Request:** "Design a character: an elderly witch who runs a launderette."

**Reading:** They have the concept. The job is specificity: who exactly is she?

**Idea:** Magic as one more household chore. She is unimpressed by it.

**Decisions:** Stop-motion puppet on a miniature set. Three-quarter view across her counter. Fluorescent tube light from above. Lilac, mint and cream with brass details.

**Prompt:**

> Stop-motion animation still: a handmade puppet character on a miniature set, with visible thumbprints in the clay and stitching on the fabric. An elderly witch who runs a launderette stands behind her counter, short and round-shouldered, in a quilted lilac housecoat, her pointed hat squashed flat under a hairnet, reading glasses on a beaded chain. She is folding a bedsheet that still glows faintly. Behind her, a row of chunky front-loading machines, one spinning with green light inside. Warm fluorescent tube light from above, soft shadows. Lilac, mint green and cream, with small brass details. Kind, practical, unbothered. Portrait 3:4.

## What the examples share

- The first words name the medium, and the next sentence places the subject in the frame.
- Each names three or four colours and says which appears once.
- Each includes one or two flaws that belong to the medium.
- The mood words come last, after the visible things that produce the mood are already in place.
- None contains a quality booster, and none says what should be absent.
